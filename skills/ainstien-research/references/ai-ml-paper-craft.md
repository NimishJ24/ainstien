# AI/ML Paper Craft

Use this guide for AI/ML research papers, drafts, literature reviews, benchmarks, tools, methods, and position pieces.

## Diagnose Contribution Type

Classify the work before writing.

### Insight Paper

Gives the field a new way to see something. The unit of value is a mental model.

Needs:

- Non-obvious, falsifiable thesis
- Sharp claim boundaries
- Evidence calibrated to claim breadth
- Serious alternatives
- Framing that changes the reader's cognitive state

### Tool Paper

Gives the field something useful. The unit of value is a working artifact.

Needs:

- Code, model, dataset, benchmark, or system that works
- Honest positioning
- Clear reason to use it over alternatives
- Practical documentation
- Reproducibility and permissive use when possible

### Synthesis Or Position Paper

Gives the field structure or a stance. The unit of value is a taxonomy, map, or argument.

Needs:

- Point of view
- Organized literature, not a list
- Exposed disagreements
- Current but durable framing
- Prescriptive recommendation when appropriate

## Cross-Cutting Tests

- **Thesis test**: Can the claim be stated in one sentence that a smart colleague may argue with?
- **Evidence-calibration audit**: Does evidence support the actual breadth of the claim?
- **Necessity test**: If this work did not exist, what downstream work would be harder or missing?
- **Cognitive-state test**: Six months later, will readers remember the idea, not just the method or number?
- **First-paragraph test**: Does the opening earn attention immediately?
- **Type-consistency test**: Do abstract, intro, contribution list, and experiments match the same contribution type?

## Anti-Patterns

- Method plus benchmark improvements with no thesis
- SOTA chase with no idea attached
- Broad claims from narrow evidence
- Related work as an unstructured citation dump
- Hidden limitations
- Reproducibility treated as optional
- A tool paper pretending to be an insight paper
- An insight paper evaluated like a tool paper

## Output

Return contribution type, thesis quality, evidence fit, paper risks, and the most useful rewrite or experiment.

# Paper Discussion

Use this guide when the user is discussing, questioning, or trying to understand a paper, section, figure, method, proof, experiment, or result.

## Purpose

Paper discussion is part of research, not side chat. The user's questions reveal what they are learning, where their mental model is weak, and which ideas are beginning to form.

## Reflective, Not Interrogative

Do not force the user through every reading-log field. Use the fields as lenses for thinking.

When a paper or excerpt is available, infer what you can:

- Title, authors, venue, and year
- Problem
- Central claim
- Method or argument
- Evidence and key result
- Assumptions and limitations
- Connections to the active journey

Ask the user only for personal or high-value reflections that cannot be inferred:

- What part felt most important or surprising?
- What confused you?
- Do you trust the evidence?
- What does this make you want to test or read next?
- How does this change your current research question?

## Discussion Capture

During discussion, capture:

- User questions
- Clarified concepts
- Remaining confusions
- Important details discovered
- Misunderstandings corrected
- Connections to other papers
- Candidate ideas sparked
- Follow-up reading or experiments
- Updates to the active research state

## Understanding Loop

For difficult sections:

1. Restate the section plainly.
2. Explain why the authors needed it.
3. Identify the assumption or mechanism.
4. Connect it to the project state.
5. Ask one useful reflective question if needed.
6. Save the discussion notes when they affect the journey.

## Output

Update or produce `discussion-notes.md` when discussion changes the user's understanding, reveals confusion, creates an idea, or affects the research state.
